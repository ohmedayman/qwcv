How to Write a Cover Letter That Gets Noticed

January 12, 2025 · 9 min read

Most applicants skip the cover letter or write a generic one — yet it's your golden chance to show personality and enthusiasm. Here's how to write one a hiring manager actually reads.

What Is a Cover Letter and Why Does It Matter?

A cover letter is a one-page document you attach with your resume when applying. The resume lists facts and achievements; the cover letter tells the story behind them — who you are, why this role, and what value you bring.

  • It shows genuine interest in the company and the role.
  • It lets you explain how your experience fits the job.
  • It highlights your personality and professional style.
  • It sets you apart from applicants who send a resume only.
Did you know? Over 70% of hiring managers read the cover letter before the resume. A strong opening can open the door to an interview.

The Ideal Cover Letter Structure

Three parts make a great letter: a strong introduction, a compelling body, and a clear closing.

  • Introduction — the first 30 seconds. State the job you're applying for, how you heard about it, and one attention-grabbing achievement.
  • Body. Explain why you fit — focus on 2-3 key points with numbers, and show how you'll add value without repeating the resume.
  • Closing. Restate interest, propose a next step (a call or meeting), thank them, and add your contact details.

Ready-Made Templates

Template 1 — For experienced professionals

Dear [Manager's name],
I'm writing to apply for the [job title] position at [company]. With over [X] years in [field] and a track record of [key achievement with numbers], I'm confident I can contribute to [company goal].
In my previous role at [company], I [achievement 1 with numbers] and [achievement 2], which led to [positive result]. My experience in [key skill] maps directly to this role, especially in [specific aspect].
I'd love to discuss how I can support your team. I'm available for an interview at your convenience.

Template 2 — For students and fresh graduates

Dear [Manager's name],
I'm a recent graduate in [major] from [university], applying for the [job title] role at [company]. My passion for [field] pushed me to focus on it throughout my studies.
During my studies, I built [number] projects using [tools], won [competition/honor], and completed an internship at [company]. I'm excited to apply my skills in a real professional environment.
Thank you for your time and consideration.

Template 3 — For returning to work

Dear [Manager's name],
I'm excited to apply for the [job title] position at [company]. After a break focused on family, I'm returning to the workforce with renewed energy.
I bring [X] years of experience in [field], where I was responsible for [responsibilities]. I also completed [courses] during my break to keep my skills current.
I'd be glad to discuss how I can contribute to your team.

Common Cover Letter Mistakes

  • One letter for every job. Tailor each letter to the specific advertisement.
  • Repeating the resume. The letter should complement it, not copy it.
  • Focusing only on yourself. Write "I can contribute to..." instead of "I'm looking for a chance".
  • Too long. Keep it to one page — around 300-400 words.
  • Typos. Proofread twice; spelling errors signal carelessness.

Professional Tips

  • Read the job ad at least three times and research the company culture.
  • Open with a bold, specific sentence — not a cliché.
  • Add measurable numbers: "boosted sales 30% in six months".
  • Read it aloud for flow, ask a friend to review, and save as a clearly named PDF.

Quick FAQ

  • Is a cover letter always required? Not always, but attaching one when not asked is a positive extra that shows interest.
  • Should I mention salary? No — leave salary discussions for the interview or offer stage.
  • Is a short letter OK? Yes. 200-300 focused, powerful words beat a padded page.
